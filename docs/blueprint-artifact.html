Executive Summary

A conversion-focused, SEO-first, full-stack technology staffing platform serving three audiences on one property: enterprise hiring managers, technology consultants, and executive decision-makers evaluating a multi-year workforce partner.

SECTION 04

Brand Positioning

Recommended positioning

Vertis Global is the enterprise technology staffing partner that delivers the same vetted talent standard onshore, offshore, or blended — so clients choose their cost structure without compromising their quality bar.

Defensible for three reasons: it is true and evidenced (200+ consultants, US and India, one four-stage process); it is a genuine gap (TEKsystems and Insight Global are US-onshore; Randstad and Adecco sell country-by-country); and it maps to a real buying decision — every enterprise program has a cost/proximity trade-off, and Vertis sells the trade-off itself.

Primary value proposition

Vetted technology consultants, delivered in 48 hours — onshore, offshore, or hybrid.

Carries what, how fast and how flexible. No adjective that cannot be proven.

SECTION 06

Navigation Architecture

A staffing site serves two audiences with opposite goals. The failure modes are well documented: burying jobs kills the largest traffic source; leading with jobs makes the site read as a job board; a 50/50 split forces a choice before credibility exists.

Solution

Asymmetric prominence with unambiguous separation. Employer content occupies the navigation's centre of gravity. The candidate path is one visually distinct, always-visible destination — impossible to miss, never competing for the same visual weight.

Six is the ceiling. Seven or more degrades scan time and forces a font-size reduction that undermines the premium register.

The right-side CTA cluster

  • Find Jobs sits left of Request Talent so the higher-value employer CTA holds the terminal position, while the candidate path still gets a persistent button — not a text link lost among nav items.
  • The live job count does real work: vitality for candidates, scale for employers, and a click magnet. Cached, revalidated every 5 minutes.
  • Outlined vs filled makes the two paths instantly separable without explanation.

SECTION 09

Job Search UX

URL is the state

Every filter, sort and page writes to the query string. Searches become shareable, back-button-correct, server-renderable and analytics-legible. Filter state must never live only in React state — that breaks sharing, SEO and the back button, and it is the most common failure in job-board builds.

Job search is a task with a completion state, not a feed. 20 per page. A "Load more" button is acceptable on mobile, provided the paginated URLs still exist and are crawlable.

The job card

  • Rate is always shown — a range, or the explicit string "Rate on application." A blank rate reads as concealment and depresses click-through.
  • Skill chips sit above the excerpt — for a technology candidate they are the highest-value scan element.
  • Job ID visible — an authenticity signal, and it lets candidates reference a role in email.
  • [URGENT] capped at 10% of live roles so the badge retains meaning.

Expired jobs return 410 Gone, not 404 — with a page explaining the role closed plus three similar live roles. This protects the Google Jobs relationship and captures the traffic.

SECTION 10

Job Application UX

Industry-average completion is roughly 35–40%, worse on mobile. Every abandoned application is a sourcing cost paid twice. Target: 65%+, achieved by removing typing rather than shortening the form arbitrarily.

Three decisions

Multi-step, not single-page — 22 fields on one screen produces immediate mobile abandonment. Resume-first, not form-first — parsing turns step 2 into confirmation rather than data entry. No account required — forced registration before applying is the single largest drop-off cause in candidate funnels.

StepFieldsRequired
1 — ResumeFile, or LinkedIn, or manual1 action
2 — Confirm details8 fields, all prefilled5
3 — Role questionsWork auth, sponsorship, availability, rate, notes, consent4

Cut at MVP

Every field must justify itself against "does a recruiter act on this in the first 48 hours?"

Address / postcode Referral source Education history Employment history Cover letter Salary history Date of birth Gender / ethnicity

Salary history is never collected — illegal to request in many US states. Voluntary EEO collection, where required, is a separate post-submission step with a clear legal notice, never mixed into the application.

Parsing must never block

Parsing fails on scanned PDFs, unusual layouts and some non-Latin scripts. On failure the flow shows "We couldn't read that automatically — please fill in a few details" and presents step 2 empty but pre-focused. The resume is stored regardless of parse success. A parse failure must never cost an application. Low-confidence fields are prefilled but marked with a subtle accent underline and a "Check this" hint.

Twelve drop-off reducers

  1. No account gate
  2. Resume-parse prefill (~12 fields removed)
  3. LinkedIn as an alternative entry
  4. Three steps with an honest time estimate
  5. Auto-save per step
  6. One abandoned-application recovery email at +24h
  7. Native mobile file picker, 56px target
  8. Invisible bot protection — no visible CAPTCHA
  9. Persistent role context in the header
  10. Honest exit handling, no dark patterns
  11. Single, clear consent with the retention period inline
  12. Correct keyboard and autocomplete on every field
Highest-trust element on the site

The recruiter card on the success screen — name, photo, direct email. It converts an anonymous submission into a relationship and measurably reduces "did that go anywhere?" support email. Every candidate also receives a closing email when a decision is made; non-response is the most-cited candidate complaint in the industry and the cheapest reputational fix available.

SECTION 11

Employer UX

Critical insight

Enterprise buyers do not convert on the first visit. Typical evaluation involves 3–6 sessions across 2–5 weeks, often by different people in the same organisation. The site must serve two modes: fast mode (urgent req — three pages, under four minutes) and slow mode (vendor evaluation — every objection needs a linkable, quotable page that gets forwarded internally and cited in scorecards). Designing for only one loses the other.

Request Talent — field logic

Two steps, requirement before contact details. Counter-intuitive and deliberate: it front-loads the part the buyer finds useful and back-loads the part that feels like a cost. A step-1 abandon still yields demand analytics, and once step 2 begins, sunk cost works in favour of completion.

What makes an industry page rank rather than read as filler

Every industry page needs a section no other page could contain. The Regulatory & Operating Context block is that section: SOX and PCI-DSS for financial services; HIPAA, GxP and CSV for life sciences; IT/OT convergence and ISA-95 for manufacturing; NERC CIP for energy. Only a firm with real sector experience can write it. Minimum 900 unique words per page, with sector-weighted role lists — not copy-pasted ones.

Worked example — Financial Services. Delivery nuance that converts and that generalist competitors cannot write: onshore is typically required for production data access; hybrid works well where offshore handles non-production and platform engineering.

SEO Architecture

The compounding asset

The job index. Every live role is a JobPosting-marked page eligible for Google Jobs. At 200–400 live roles refreshed continuously, this produces sustained organic volume no amount of marketing-page optimisation can match — and it lifts domain authority for the commercial pages.

Three rules that decide whether Google Jobs works

  1. validThrough on every posting, always in the future. Expired postings are dropped, and repeated violations damage the whole domain's standing.
  2. Closed roles return 410 or leave the sitemap immediately — on-demand revalidation, not a 6-hour wait.
  3. baseSalary wherever disclosed materially improves ranking and click-through. This is a commercial argument for publishing rates on more postings.

Programmatic SEO — with hard index gating

FamilyPatternIndex rule
Skill/jobs/skill/[skill]/≥3 live roles
Category/jobs/category/[category]/≥3 live roles
Location/jobs/location/[country]/[city]/≥5 live roles
Skill × Location/jobs/[skill]-jobs-in-[city]/≥3 roles AND ≥200 unique words

The index gate is the most important rule in this section. Below threshold a page still renders for users but carries noindex, follow and canonicals to its parent. Without this gate a job board generates thousands of near-empty pages, Google classifies the site as thin-content, and rankings collapse across the whole domain. Every major job board has learned this expensively.

Cannibalisation control

One page owns each primary term. Capability pages own skill terms; solution pages own engagement-model terms; the delivery-models page owns geography terms. This is why the three-axis IA matters commercially, not just conceptually.

Assign conversion values

lead_submitted 500 · capability_overview_downloaded 100 · gated_content_downloaded 50 · application_submitted 15 · resume_submitted 10 · job_alert_created 5 · newsletter_subscribed 3. Without values, GA4 treats a newsletter signup and an enterprise lead identically, which makes every channel report misleading.

  • Server-side mirroring of the four primary conversions via the Measurement Protocol, so ad-blockers and ITP do not silently undercount the events the business is measured on.
  • No PII in analytics — ever. Internal IDs only. Also a GDPR requirement.
  • UTM discipline captured into leads on submission, so offline sales outcomes attribute back to campaigns.

Integration principle

Every integration is behind a feature flag and degrades gracefully. An email provider outage must never block an application submission — queue and retry. Webhooks are signature-verified and idempotent; duplicate deliveries are expected and must be harmless.

Common weaknesses — Vertis's openings

The differentiation summary

Vertis cannot out-scale Kelly, Randstad or Adecco, and should not try. It cannot out-brand TEKsystems on recognition alone. What it can do is be the most specific, most transparent and most operationally credible firm in the set. Named platforms instead of categories. A published vetting process instead of a claim. Rate ranges instead of "competitive." A commercial model on the website instead of behind a sales call. Specificity is the strategy — every design and content decision in this document serves it.
